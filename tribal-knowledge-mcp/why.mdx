# why i thought of this idea

every organization has this invisible layer of knowledge that nobody writes down. a senior engineer decides "we're not using library X because of Y" at 2pm on a tuesday, and that decision lives in their head- maybe in a slack thread somewhere, probably nowhere useful.

when they leave, or when someone new joins, that context just evaporates. and the new person either makes the same mistake, or spends hours asking around trying to understand why things are the way they are.

we call it tribal knowledge- the informal, undocumented understanding that experienced people carry around and pass on through hallway conversations and pairing sessions. it's hard to capture, harder to search, and basically impossible to surface at the right moment.

the usual answer is "put it in the wiki"- but nobody reads wikis at the point of decision. you read the wiki when you're onboarding, maybe. you don't check the wiki when you're mid-task deciding which library to use or how to structure an api call.

so the idea was- what if this knowledge lived where engineers actually work? what if your ai coding agent could just query it as a tool, right when you're making a decision? instead of a wiki that collects dust, you get context that surfaces when it's relevant.

that's the core of it- make tribal knowledge a first-class citizen in the development workflow by giving it an mcp server.
